# Exam Pattern and Strategy

## Preliminary examination

| Section | Questions | Marks | Sectional time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Reasoning Ability | 40 | 40 | 25 minutes |
| Quantitative Aptitude | 40 | 40 | 20 minutes |
| Total | 80 | 80 | 45 minutes |

There is a penalty of one-fourth of the marks assigned to a question for each wrong answer. An unanswered question has no penalty.

## What the pattern means

- The timers are separate. Unused Reasoning time cannot be transferred to Quant.
- Reasoning allows about 37.5 seconds per question on average.
- Quant allows about 30 seconds per question on average.
- Some questions take only 10-20 seconds; puzzles, DI and arithmetic may take much longer.
- Question selection is therefore essential.

## Recommended first-pass strategy

### Reasoning: 25 minutes

1. First 7-9 minutes: inequality, syllogism, direction, blood relation, ranking, series and other direct questions.
2. Remaining time: choose the easiest puzzle or seating set first.
3. Leave a set if its structure is still unclear after roughly 60-90 seconds.

### Quant: 20 minutes

1. First 6-8 minutes: approximation/simplification, number series and quadratic equations when present.
2. Next: select the easiest DI set.
3. Use the remaining time for arithmetic questions with familiar methods.

The real order must be adjusted after mock-test data shows which topics are fastest and most accurate for you.

## Mock analysis categories

Classify every lost mark as one of these:

- Concept error
- Calculation error
- Misread condition
- Poor question selection
- Time-management error
- Blind guess

The purpose of a mock is not merely to obtain a score. It is to identify and remove repeatable errors.
