# Reasoning Ability - Complete Topic Map

## Priority A: direct and relatively fast topics

Study these first because they often provide marks without consuming puzzle-level time.

- Inequality
  - Direct inequality
  - Coded inequality
  - Either-or conclusions
- Syllogism
  - Basic conclusions
  - Possibility cases
  - Only/few/some statements
- Direction and distance
- Blood relations and family tree
- Order and ranking
- Alphanumeric and symbol series
- Word/number pairing and meaningful-word questions
- Basic coding-decoding

## Priority B: arrangements and puzzles

- Linear seating
  - Single row
  - North/south facing
  - Parallel rows
  - Uncertain number of persons
- Circular seating
  - Facing centre/outside
  - Mixed facing
- Square/rectangular seating
- Floor and flat puzzles
- Box/stack puzzles
- Day, date and month scheduling
- Age-based puzzles
- Designation and profession puzzles
- Comparison and ordering puzzles
- Double-variable puzzles

## Priority C: conditional and miscellaneous topics

- Input-output
- Data sufficiency
- Statement and conclusion
- Statement and assumption
- Cause and effect
- Course of action
- Logical reasoning miscellaneous

These topics do not appear with identical weight in every shift. Prepare them after the recurring direct topics and core puzzle formats.

## Learning order

1. Inequality
2. Syllogism
3. Direction and distance
4. Blood relation
5. Order and ranking
6. Alphanumeric series
7. Coding-decoding
8. Linear seating
9. Circular seating
10. Floor/flat and box puzzles
11. Scheduling and designation puzzles
12. Input-output and data sufficiency
13. Mixed exam-level sets

## Mastery test for each topic

A topic is not complete until you can:

- Explain its rules without notes.
- Solve basic questions with at least 90% accuracy.
- Solve a timed mixed set without confusing its conditions.
- Identify when a question or puzzle should be skipped.
