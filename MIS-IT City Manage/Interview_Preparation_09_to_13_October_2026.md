## 3. Understand the job in simple language

**MIS = Management Information System.** It is a combination of people, processes and technology that turns records into information managers can use.

Example: sanitation staff provide ward collection records. The MIS person checks dates, ward IDs, units and totals, prepares a summary and flags gaps. The officer can then identify where service needs attention.

**Data** is a raw value: “Ward 2: 260 households covered.” **Information** gives context: “Ward 2 covered 260 of 300 households: 86.67%.” A **decision** is the next action: check the unserved households and coordinate corrective work.

Suggested role responsibilities to understand, subject to the municipality's actual assignment:

- Collect and maintain municipal programme records.
- Check completeness, consistency and supporting evidence.
- Prepare Excel summaries, charts and periodic reports.
- Update authorised MIS portals after checks and approval.
- Organise evidence such as registers, photographs and document references.
- Coordinate with field staff and officers to resolve discrepancies.
- Help with routine computer, printer, connectivity and file problems.
- Track complaints, deadlines and pending actions where assigned.
- Protect official data and maintain recoverable backups.

**A strong answer:** “I see this as a data and coordination role. I would collect records, validate them against evidence, prepare useful reports, update approved portals and help staff with routine IT problems. Accurate reporting should help the municipality improve service.”

Gujarati: “આ પદમાં નગરપાલિકાના ડેટાનું સંકલન, ચકાસણી, રિપોર્ટિંગ અને અધિકૃત પોર્ટલ પર અપડેટ મહત્વનું છે. હું ફીલ્ડ સ્ટાફ અને અધિકારીઓ સાથે સંકલન કરીને સાચો અને સમયસર રિપોર્ટ તૈયાર કરીશ.”

## 4. Municipal and Swachh Bharat basics

### Facts to understand

SBM-U means Swachh Bharat Mission–Urban. Its first phase launched on 2 October 2014; phase 2.0 launched on 1 October 2021 with a vision of garbage-free cities and sustained sanitation. Source: [official mission overview](https://stagingwebsite.sbmurban.org/aboutUs).

ULB means Urban Local Body. Rajpipla Nagarpalika is the municipality named in your notice. Its official contact directory places it in Narmada district. Source: [district directory](https://narmada.nic.in/public-utility/rajpipla-municipality/).

The official GFC process includes completing the city profile and MIS, uploading evidence/declarations, and third-party verification. This supports preparing for data accuracy and evidence handling. Source: [official GFC dashboard and application process](https://stagingwebsite.sbmurban.org/gfc-result-dashboard-2024-25).

### Vocabulary: learn the meaning, not only the full form

- **SWM:** solid waste management: collection, transport, sorting, processing and appropriate disposal.
- **Source segregation:** separating waste where it is generated. Wet waste includes food/organic waste; dry recyclable waste includes suitable paper, plastic and metal. Other waste streams need their specified handling; do not assume all waste fits two categories.
- **D2D:** door-to-door collection.
- **MRF:** material recovery facility, where dry waste can be sorted for recovery/recycling.
- **Composting:** treatment of suitable biodegradable waste to produce compost.
- **Legacy waste:** previously accumulated waste at old dumpsites.
- **TPD:** tonnes per day. Always distinguish kilograms, tonnes, daily rates and monthly totals.
- **IEC:** information, education and communication; for example, awareness about segregation.
- **KPI:** key performance indicator, a defined measure used to track progress.
- **GIS:** geographic information system; links information to locations.
- **Geotag:** location information linked to a photo or record. A geotag alone does not prove the whole service was completed.
- **SLA:** service level agreement; a defined service standard or deadline where applicable. Do not invent a complaint deadline.

### Sanitation certification terms

Learn the distinction: **ODF** concerns freedom from open defecation; **ODF+** adds functional, maintained community/public toilets and no open urination; **ODF++** adds safe management and treatment of sewage/faecal sludge; **Water+** concerns collection, treatment and sustainable reuse of used water. These are brief explanations, not the full certification conditions. Source: [official SBM dashboard descriptions](https://stagingwebsite.sbmurban.org/).

**Swachh Survekshan** is an urban cleanliness assessment. **GFC** is Garbage Free Cities star-rating certification. Understand that evidence and verification matter. Exact survey indicators, scores and protocols change by edition; check the applicable current documents before quoting a scoring rule. Do not memorise the 2023 marks as if they apply in 2026. Source for the assessment concept: [official Swachh Survekshan site, historical 2023 edition](https://ss2023.sbmurban.org/).

### A. Enter this dataset

Create a sheet named `Practice`. Put the following into cells A1:H7. If pasting puts everything in one column, use Data → Text to Columns → Delimited → Comma. This is inline sample data, not an additional file you must download.

```text
Date,Ward,Households,Covered,Segregating,WasteKg,ProcessedKg,Complaints
2026-10-09,1,200,180,140,500,400,5
```

For this exercise, `Covered` means households served that day; `Segregating` is a subset of covered households; `WasteKg` is waste collected that day; `ProcessedKg` is the portion of that day's collected waste processed. In actual reporting, define each field according to the required reporting format: processing may include earlier stock and collection is not necessarily generation.

The same ward population repeats on two dates. Summing `Households` across both dates gives household-day observations, not the city's unique households. This is a useful interview example of why reporting period and denominator matter.

### B. Basic formulas

Put `Coverage` in I1 and `SegregationAmongCovered` in J1.

```excel
=IF(C2=0,"Check denominator",D2/C2)
=IF(D2=0,"Check denominator",E2/D2)
```

Enter the first formula in I2, the second in J2 and fill down to row 7. Format valid numbers as percentages. Do not multiply by 100 as well as using Percentage format.

Useful practice formulas:

```excel
=SUM(F2:F7)
=AVERAGE(F2:F7)
=MAX(H2:H7)
=MIN(H2:H7)
=COUNT(F2:F7)
=COUNTA(B2:B7)
=COUNTIF(B2:B7,2)
=SUMIF(B2:B7,2,F2:F7)
=SUMIFS(F2:F7,B2:B7,2)
=COUNTIFS(B2:B7,2,H2:H7,">5")
```

In order: total waste 3610 kg; mean waste per row approximately 601.67 kg; maximum complaints 8; minimum 3; numeric count 6; nonempty ward count 6; two Ward 2 rows; Ward 2 waste 1380 kg; again 1380 kg; two Ward 2 rows with complaints above 5.

`COUNT` counts numbers; `COUNTA` counts nonempty cells. `SUMIF` handles one condition; `SUMIFS` can handle several. In `SUMIFS`, the sum range comes first and ranges must align. Source: [Microsoft SUMIFS documentation](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/functions/sumifs-function).

For two conditions, put an actual Excel date for 9 October 2026 in L2, then use:

```excel
=SUMIFS(F2:F7,B2:B7,2,A2:A7,L2)
```

Expected result: **700 kg**, provided column A and L2 contain matching date values. A date stored as text may not match a real Excel date. Check types when a formula unexpectedly returns zero.

### C. Lookup a ward name

Put ward IDs 1, 2, 3 in N2:N4 and fictional names Ward One, Ward Two, Ward Three in O2:O4.

```excel
=XLOOKUP(B2,$N$2:$N$4,$O$2:$O$4,"Not found")
=IFERROR(VLOOKUP(B2,$N$2:$O$4,2,FALSE),"Not found")
```

These are alternatives. XLOOKUP is unavailable in Excel 2016 and 2019; use VLOOKUP where necessary. `FALSE` requests an exact VLOOKUP match; the lookup ID must be in the first column of its lookup range. `$` locks a reference when copying a formula. Source for XLOOKUP behaviour and version availability: [Microsoft XLOOKUP documentation](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/excel/functions/xlookup-function).

Do not use IFERROR to hide genuine reporting failures. A zero may be a real value, while “Not found” or “Check denominator” tells you to investigate.

### D. Sort, filter, clean and validate

1. Save a raw copy before making changes.
2. Select the complete dataset before sorting, so columns stay together.
3. Filter Ward 2; verify that two rows remain.
4. Check dates and numeric types. Keep phone numbers and IDs with leading zeroes as text where appropriate.
5. Flag missing mandatory fields and unexpected values.
6. For this exercise, check `Covered <= Households`, `Segregating <= Covered`, and nonnegative quantities.
7. Review potential duplicates using the expected key. Here it is Date + Ward; real systems may need route or shift as well.
8. Do not delete repeated wards across different dates; those are legitimate records.

For untidy names, `=TRIM(K2)` removes ordinary extra spaces; it does not resolve every possible hidden Unicode character. Use standard ward IDs and authorised master lists. Drop-down validation can reduce spelling variants. Conditional formatting can highlight missing values or low coverage, but it does not replace checking source documents.

### E. Create a PivotTable and chart

Select A1:H7 → Insert → PivotTable. Put Ward in Rows, WasteKg and ProcessedKg in Values, and Date in Filters. Check that numeric fields summarise by **Sum**, not Count. Filter to 9 October and check total collected waste is 1800 kg. Refresh after changes to the underlying data.

A PivotTable summarises records without rewriting the original rows. Microsoft explains choosing Sum, Count or Average in [its PivotTable documentation](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/sum-values-in-a-pivottable).

Make a simple column chart comparing collected and processed kilograms by ward for one date. Give it a title, date and units. Do not mix kilograms and percentages on an unexplained axis.

### F. Percentages you should be able to explain

- Collection coverage: covered households / eligible households for the same period and scope.
- Segregation: use the specified denominator; in this exercise, segregating / covered households.
- Complaint closure: resolved / total received in the defined cohort. Real reports may also include previous backlog; state the basis.
- Waste processing: use the official indicator's definition. Processing / generation differs from processing / collection.

Do not average ward percentages when ward sizes differ. Calculate the combined numerator divided by the combined denominator.

## 6. Accurate MIS reporting and portal work

### The workflow to remember

**Collect → validate → reconcile → obtain approval → submit → retain evidence → follow up.**

Original example: field staff send a ward report, a collection register and a weighing record. You check date, ward, units and completeness, compare totals, clarify differences and record corrections. Prepare a summary for review. Upload approved figures through the authorised account, then retain the acknowledgement and evidence reference.

Before submission, check:

- Are the period, ward IDs and units correct?
- Is the data complete? Are missing records marked as pending rather than zero?
- Are totals supported by registers, weighing slips or other applicable source records?
- Are repeated records legitimate or duplicate entries?
- Has a large change been explained?
- Does the upload agree with the reviewed report?
- Are photographs/documents relevant, legible and linked to the right activity?
- Has the designated officer approved where required?
- Have you saved the submission reference and final version?

### Report template you can describe in the interview

Use a title such as “Daily Sanitation MIS — 9 October 2026 — Practice Only”. Include reporting period, data source, wards covered, collection and segregation indicators, waste quantities with units, pending complaints, missing data, exceptions, action owner and deadline, and prepared/reviewed details. Keep the officer's summary short; retain the detailed evidence separately.

Illustrative action note: “Ward 2 coverage is lower than the other practice wards on 10 October. Confirm the unserved locations with field staff and record the explanation before recommending follow-up.” Do not infer a vehicle failure from a number alone.

### File and evidence management

Use names such as `2026-10-09_Ward02_Collection_Reviewed_v1.xlsx`. Maintain separate raw, working and reviewed versions. An evidence index can contain date, ward, activity, file reference, source and review status. A correction log should record the original value, revised value, reason, supporting record and reviewer.

Use approved storage and access controls. Avoid placing citizen records in public links or personal accounts. Keep only the personal information needed for the authorised purpose. Confirm a file opens after saving/exporting and test recovery through the approved backup procedure.

### If you have never used a government portal

Say: “I have not used that particular portal yet. I understand the reporting process: confirm definitions, validate the data, obtain the required approval, enter it carefully and retain the acknowledgement. I would learn the portal through its authorised guidance and supervised practice.”

Never claim portal experience that you do not have. Publicly available SBM pages can help you understand reporting; do not try to access municipal accounts without authorisation.

## 7. Basic IT and office skills

### Concepts in plain language

- Hardware is the physical equipment; software is the operating system and applications.
- RAM is working memory; storage keeps saved files. An SSD is storage, not RAM.
- An operating system manages hardware and applications.
- A LAN connects devices within a local area; the internet connects networks more widely.
- An IP address identifies a network interface; DNS helps translate names to IP addresses.
- A switch connects devices in a network; a router forwards traffic between networks.
- HTTP is a web protocol; HTTPS adds encrypted transport using TLS. HTTPS alone does not prove a site is trustworthy.
- A browser opens websites; a search engine helps find them.
- A database stores structured records; a primary key uniquely identifies a row.
- A PDF is useful for fixed-layout sharing; CSV stores plain tabular text and does not preserve workbook formulas, charts or multiple sheets.

### Troubleshooting method

Ask what failed, when it began, what changed, who is affected, and the exact error. Check simple causes first. Try one reversible change at a time within your access level. Record what you tried, escalate with useful evidence if needed, and verify the service works afterwards.

**No internet:** check whether one website or every website fails; whether only one device or the whole office is affected; Wi-Fi/Ethernet connection; another permitted browser/site; network settings and the approved IT support route. Do not reset shared equipment during other staff's work without coordination.

**Printer not printing:** check power, paper, error display and connection; correct printer selection; offline status; stuck jobs; and a test page. If a driver or shared printer service needs attention, use authorised support. Verify with the user after recovery.

**Slow computer:** check free space, unusually busy applications, startup workload and updates; save work before restart; use the approved security scan if appropriate. Avoid downloading unknown “speed booster” software.

**Portal upload fails:** check the authorised URL, network, session, required fields, file type/size, readable evidence and exact error. Save the report and timestamped error evidence. Check whether submission already succeeded before retrying. Escalate before the deadline with the affected report and error details.

**Backup:** use the office's approved backup location and schedule, protected access and periodic restore checks. A successful copy is not proof of a working backup until recovery is checked.

**Phishing:** verify unexpected senders and requests through a known contact route; do not share passwords/OTPs; report suspicious messages. Use official account recovery instead of asking another person for credentials.

- PDF/scanning: ensure pages are upright, readable and complete; use approved tools to combine or resize documents when needed.
- Presentation: explain one chart with its period, units, main issue and next action.

## 9. Interview question bank with short model answers

These are original practice questions, not leaked or official questions. Adapt answers to your experience. Most answers should take 30–60 seconds; scenarios can take up to 90 seconds.

### Role and municipal basics — practise on Day 1

**1. What is MIS?** A system of people, processes and technology that converts data into useful management information. In a municipality it can show service coverage, pending work and trends.

**2. What is the difference between data and information?** Data is a raw record; information is data with context. “180 households served” becomes useful when compared with the 200 eligible households and the correct date.

**3. What would you do as City Manager MIS/IT?** Collect and validate records, prepare reports, update authorised systems after required review, organise evidence, coordinate corrections and support routine IT tasks assigned to me.

**4. Why is accurate data important?** Wrong figures can hide service gaps and lead to poor decisions. I would use traceable source records, checks and review before submission.

**5. What do you know about SBM-U 2.0?** It is an urban sanitation mission with a garbage-free-city vision. For this role I would focus on reliable reporting and evidence about collection, segregation, waste handling and sanitation activities.

**6. What is source segregation?** Separating waste at its point of generation so suitable streams can be collected and processed appropriately. The reporting definitions must match the applicable format.

**7. What is the difference between Swachh Survekshan and GFC?** Swachh Survekshan assesses urban cleanliness; GFC is a star-rating certification process. I would refer to the applicable edition's protocols for exact criteria.

**8. What do you know about Rajpipla?** The advertised employer is Rajpipla Nagarpalika in Narmada district. I have checked its official contact directory. I would learn ward-level operations and verified service data rather than guess local figures.

### Excel and data — practise on Day 2

**9. Which Excel skills are useful here?** Sort/filter, totals, conditional formulas, lookups, validation, PivotTables and simple charts. Explain one that you have actually practised.

**10. SUMIF versus SUMIFS?** SUMIF sums records using one condition. SUMIFS can use multiple conditions, such as ward and date, with aligned ranges.

**11. Why use a PivotTable?** It quickly summarises many records by ward or period. I check field types, aggregation and filters and refresh it when source data changes.

**12. What is data validation?** Checks that entries match required types or rules; for example, an authorised ward list and a valid reporting date. Spreadsheet validation helps prevent errors but source verification remains necessary.

**13. How do you find duplicates?** First define the expected unique key. Then flag matching keys and inspect source records. I would not delete repeated ward names if they represent different dates or shifts.

**14. Missing data versus zero?** Zero means confirmed absence of the measured quantity. Missing means unknown or not received. I mark it pending and seek the record rather than report zero.

**15. How do you calculate overall coverage?** Total covered households divided by total eligible households for the same scope and period. Averaging ward percentages can be wrong when ward sizes differ.

**16. A formula returns zero unexpectedly. What do you check?** Criteria, selected range, date/text types, spaces, numeric types and filters. I compare a small manually checked example before trusting the whole report.

**17. Field and portal figures disagree. What do you do?** Compare scope, dates, units, source records and versions. Clarify with the responsible staff, document the correction and update only through the approved process.

### IT and judgement — practise on Day 3

**18. The portal is down near a deadline.** Save the validated report, record the error and time, check scope/connectivity, notify the supervisor and authorised support, and use only an approved alternative if available. Preserve evidence and acknowledgement; do not backdate submission.

**19. Someone asks you to inflate a figure.** Explain that figures need source evidence. Present the actual records and discrepancy, seek clarification through the supervisory route and keep an appropriate record. Do not fabricate data.

**20. A citizen complaint has no clear location.** Politely request the minimum details needed, record the complaint, route it to the responsible staff and track the next action under the prescribed process.

**21. A complaint is marked resolved but the citizen disagrees.** Review closure evidence, coordinate verification, and reopen or escalate according to the system's process. A status field alone is not proof of resolution.

**22. How would you protect municipal data?** Use authorised accounts and storage, appropriate access, protected backups and verified recipients. Do not share credentials or unnecessary citizen information.

**23. What is a backup?** A protected recoverable copy of data. I would follow the approved backup schedule and test restoration, not just check that a copy exists.

**24. The office printer stops working.** Check power, paper, errors, connection, printer selection and queue; try a test print; escalate driver/shared-service issues within the authorised process and confirm recovery.

**25. Staff are late sending records.** Use a simple standard template and clear deadline, identify the reason, send reminders, flag pending records and escalate early. Never invent the missing entries to finish the report.

**26. How will you teach staff a reporting format?** Demonstrate a short example, explain required fields and common errors, let them practise, then provide a brief checklist and follow-up support.

**27. Two urgent tasks arrive together.** Clarify deadlines and impact, agree priorities with the supervisor, divide the work into steps and communicate any risk early. Keep accuracy checks even under time pressure.

**28. How can technology improve cleanliness work?** Consistent ward records and location-linked complaints can help identify repeat gaps and track actions. A simple reliable process should come before proposing new software.

**29. What would you do in your first month?** Learn current reporting requirements, access controls and source records; check missing data and evidence; standardise templates with approval; establish review, submission and follow-up routines; share the main issues with the officer.

**30. You discover an error after submission.** Tell the designated officer promptly, document the error and supported correction, use the approved correction process and retain the revision trail. Investigate why it happened to prevent repetition.

### Personal answers — practise on Days 1 and 4

**31. Tell us about yourself.** Use section 8; keep it relevant and approximately one minute.

**32. Describe your experience.** Give exact dates, responsibilities, tools and one real example. Be ready to show supporting certificates.

**33. Why should we select you?** Link two demonstrable skills to the work and give evidence, not just adjectives.

**34. Can you work with field staff and officers?** Describe a real coordination example. Explain that clear formats, respectful follow-up and accurate records help teams work together.

**35. What if you do not know the software?** Be honest about current knowledge and explain how you learn: approved guidance, supervised practice, a small checked task and feedback.

**36. Do you have any questions for us?** Ask one useful question: “What are the main MIS reports and deadlines expected in the first month?” or “Which portals and reporting formats does this role use?”

## 11. Basic SQL: optional supporting preparation

SQL queries structured data. Learn SELECT (retrieve), WHERE (filter), ORDER BY (sort), GROUP BY (group totals) and COUNT/SUM (aggregate). WHERE filters rows before aggregation; HAVING filters groups. Primary keys identify records and foreign keys link related records. Source for query clauses: [PostgreSQL SELECT documentation](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-select.html); [basic querying tutorial](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/tutorial-select.html).

The following are original examples for a hypothetical table `ward_daily` with fields `report_date`, `ward_id`, `waste_kg`, `processed_kg`, `complaints`. They are learning examples, not instructions to access a municipal database.

```sql
-- Retrieve selected columns for one reporting date.
SELECT ward_id, waste_kg, complaints
FROM ward_daily
WHERE report_date = '2026-10-09'
ORDER BY complaints DESC;

-- Sum waste by ward across the available records.
SELECT ward_id, SUM(waste_kg) AS total_waste_kg
FROM ward_daily
GROUP BY ward_id;

-- Find repeated date + ward combinations for review.
SELECT report_date, ward_id, COUNT(*) AS record_count
FROM ward_daily
GROUP BY report_date, ward_id
HAVING COUNT(*) > 1;
```

For the sample: the first query starts with Ward 2, eight complaints; the second gives Ward 1 **1020**, Ward 2 **1380**, Ward 3 **1210 kg**; the third returns no rows if the six practice records are entered once. Repeated keys are a signal to inspect the data, not automatic proof that a row should be deleted.

If asked about JOIN: it combines related tables using matching keys, such as a ward master and daily records. INNER JOIN keeps matches; LEFT JOIN can retain daily records even when the master match is missing. You can explain the concept without claiming database administration experience.
